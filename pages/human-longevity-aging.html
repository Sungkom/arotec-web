ADI: Aging Dynamic
Index

The central index of human aging

ADI integrates intrinsic susceptibility, exposome-driven biological impact, and aging resilience to determine the direction and velocity of aging.

EBII: The Primary Driver and Key Determinant of ADS

EBII

Exposome Biological Impact Index

The strongest factor influencing ADI

EBII quantifies the cumulative biological impact of all relevant exposures.

  • Inflammation
  • Oxidative stress
  • Metabolic stress
  • Molecular / cellular disruption
  • Susceptibility
  • Cumulative exposure burden
EBII has the greatest weight and directly drives ADI
IP: Intrinsic Pressure

Internal susceptibility

  • Genetics
  • Epigenetic predisposition
  • Sex & developmental programming
  • Baseline physiology
  • Innate vulnerability
AR: Aging Resilience

The protective modulator

  • Repair capacity
  • Recovery capacity
  • Adaptation
  • Functional reserve
  • Homeostatic stability
  • Stress tolerance

Section 02

ADI Determines Aging Trajectory

ADI DeterminesAging Trajectory

Low ADI Healthy Aging

  • Strong resilience
  • Minimal biological drift
  • Preserved function
  • High quality of life

Moderate ADI Adaptive Aging

  • Compensated decline
  • Moderate dysfunction
  • Increased vulnerability
  • Declining reserve

High ADI Accelerated Aging

  • Loss of resilience
  • Biological dysregulation
  • Rapid functional decline
  • High risk of disease / disability

ADI indicates the direction and velocity of an individual's aging process.
