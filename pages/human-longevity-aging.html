Skin and Brain Communicate in Both Directions

The skin-brain axis describes bidirectional neuroimmune and neuroendocrine communication between skin, peripheral nerves, spinal pathways, immune mediators, hormones, and the brain.

Afferent

Skin to Brain

Cutaneous, immune, damage-associated, microbial, and metabolic signals travel through peripheral nerve endings, A-delta fibers, C fibers, dorsal root ganglion, and spinal pathways.

  • Touch, itch, pain, temperature, UV, and injury signals
  • Cytokines, chemokines, histamine, DAMPs, ROS, and alarmins
  • Microbial metabolites, PAMPs, and environmental microbiota signals
Center

Brain Regulation Centers

The prefrontal cortex, anterior cingulate cortex, insula, amygdala, hippocampus, and hypothalamus coordinate emotion, stress appraisal, autonomic control, neuroendocrine rhythm, and sleep.

Efferent

Brain to Skin

Central regulation sends outputs through the HPA axis, autonomic nervous system, neuroendocrine mediators, neuroimmune signals, hormones, cytokines, and growth factors.

  • Barrier function and immune regulation
  • Oxidative and microbiome balance
  • Neuroendocrine balance and ECM remodeling
02

Skin Homeostasis Is Maintained by Interconnected Systems

Healthy skin depends on the balance of barrier function, immunity, oxidative control, extracellular matrix integrity, microbiome diversity, and neuroendocrine regulation.

SHS Skin Homeostasis Status

Barrier Homeostasis

Stratum corneum integrity, tight junctions, filaggrin, lipids, and pH balance.

Immune Homeostasis

Langerhans cells, mast cells, macrophages, T cell balance, and immune tolerance.

Neuroendocrine Homeostasis

Cutaneous HPA axis, neurotransmitter receptors, local neuropeptides, and neuroimmune crosstalk.

Oxidative Homeostasis

ROS control, antioxidant defense, SOD, CAT, GPx, and NRF2 signaling.

Microbiome Homeostasis

Commensal diversity, colonization resistance, and microbial metabolites.

ECM Homeostasis

Collagen, elastin, proteoglycans, fibroblast function, and ECM remodeling.

03

Feedback Loops Can Amplify Stress or Restore Balance

The skin-brain axis can create either a stress-inflammation cycle or a resilience-repair cycle.

Negative Feedback Loop

Stress-Inflammation Cycle

  1. Stress and HPA axis activation
  2. Cortisol increase and barrier disruption
  3. Oxidative stress and inflammation
  4. Afferent stress signals and amplified imbalance

Positive Feedback Loop

Resilience-Repair Cycle

  1. Positive emotion and social support
  2. Parasympathetic activation and higher vagal tone
  3. Anti-inflammatory signaling
  4. Restored skin homeostasis and improved mood

The Four Components of Skin Aging Dynamics

EBII

Exposome Biological Impact Index

Conceptually quantifies total biological impact of exposome-related signals on the skin.

Higher EBII may increase biological burden.
SHS

Skin Homeostasis Status

Represents protective balance across barrier, immune, oxidative, ECM, microbiome, and neuroendocrine systems.

Strong SHS can buffer disruption.
SAF

Skin Aging Amplification Factor

Genetic susceptibility, epigenetic drift, chronic inflammation, hormonal imbalance, oxidative stress, glycation, psychosocial stress, and lifestyle factors may amplify aging burden.

Higher SAF may raise SADI.
SADI

Skin Aging Dynamic Index

An integrated conceptual index representing skin aging burden and trajectory.

SADI = EBII × SAF / SHS

07

Skin Aging Can Follow a Vicious or Virtuous Cycle

Vicious Cycle

Positive Damage Feedback

EBII up → dSHS up → SAF up → SADI up

Exposome-driven burden can amplify skin aging. Chronic inflammation, oxidative stress, and cellular senescence may reinforce damage.

Virtuous Cycle

Resilience Loop

Lower EBII → dSHS down → SAF down → SADI down

Preserved homeostasis can buffer aging progression. Repair, anti-inflammatory tone, and cellular renewal sustain resilience.

08

SADI Defines a Skin Aging Continuum

SADI can be used as a conceptual lens to describe progressive decline in skin homeostasis and biological resilience.

Low SADI

Homeostatic Skin

Intact barrier, balanced microbiome, optimal ECM turnover, normal neuroendocrine signaling, and efficient repair.

Moderate SADI

Adaptive Aging Skin

Compensated stress, mild oxidative load, early ECM remodeling, and delayed recovery.

High SADI

Accelerated Aging Skin

Persistent oxidative stress, chronic low-grade inflammation, reduced repair capacity, and barrier fragility.

Very High SADI

Homeostasis-Collapsed Skin

Severe homeostatic failure, cellular senescence accumulation, ECM breakdown, and delayed wound healing.

09

ADI: Aging Dynamic Index

The central index of human aging. ADI integrates exposome-driven biological impact and time-intrinsic aging processes to describe the direction and velocity of aging.

EBII ADI = EBII × AF AF

EBII reflects cumulative biological impact. AF represents intrinsic time-dependent processes that may amplify EBII.
