SADI-Defined Skin Aging Continuum

Progressive decline of skin homeostasis and biological resilience

Stage 1
Homeostatic Skin

Low SADI
  • Intact barrier
  • Balanced microbiome
  • Optimal ECM turnover
  • Normal neuroendocrine signaling
  • Efficient cellular repair

Phenotype: smooth texture, uniform tone, high resilience

Stage 2
Adaptive Aging Skin

Moderate SADI
  • Compensated stress
  • Mild oxidative load
  • Early ECM remodeling
  • Mild inflammatory tone

Phenotype: mild dryness, early fine lines, delayed recovery

Stage 3
Accelerated Aging Skin

High SADI
  • Persistent oxidative stress
  • Chronic low-grade inflammation
  • Reduced repair capacity
  • Barrier fragility

Phenotype: wrinkles, uneven pigmentation, reduced elasticity, sensitivity

Stage 4
Homeostasis-Collapsed Skin

Very High SADI
  • Severe homeostatic failure
  • Cellular senescence accumulation
  • ECM breakdown
  • Neuroimmune dysregulation

Phenotype: deep wrinkles, skin thinning, chronic inflammation, delayed wound healing

Neurocosmetics

Science of Brain-Skin Communication

Neurocosmetics act on multiple targets and pathways to modulate neuroimmune responses, restore skin balance, and enhance overall skin health and well-being.

Neurocosmetic Mechanisms

1

Receptor Binding

Specific ligands bind to neuronal receptors on skin cells, initiating a signal.

2

Signal Activation

Binding triggers an intracellular cascade, converting the external signal into an internal response.

3

Cellular Response

The signal leads to positive cellular changes such as increased collagen production, improved hydration, and enhanced barrier function.

Neurocosmetics work by communicating with skin cells to help skin adapt, protect, and maintain a healthy, youthful appearance.
