Why Skin Feel
Matters

Skincare experience begins before efficacy can be perceived.

From first contact, the skin senses richness, weight, spreadability, glide, absorption and after-feel. Together, these tactile cues shape perceived quality, comfort and willingness to continue use.

THE SAME FORMULA CAN FEEL DIFFERENT

  • Skin condition
  • Age
  • Sebum level
  • Stratum corneum condition
  • Application area
  • Application method
  • Climate
  • Temperature
  • Humidity
  • Daily environment

A formula that feels heavy in a hot, humid climate may feel appropriately nourishing in a cool, dry environment. Dry and oily skin may also experience the same formula very differently.

Two Dimensions
of Skin Feel

To make skin feel more objective, comparable and applicable, the A&S research team established two proprietary relative indices.

LFI and PGI are complementary, independent dimensions. They describe sensory character, not better or worse.

Understanding PGI
Predicted Glide Index

PGI estimates relative skin glide from device readings calibrated against 1–7 sensory glide ratings. It is a predictive sensory index, not a direct friction measurement.

SKIN SENSORY STANDARD

How We Measure

A controlled test-and-control protocol

The test area and an untreated control area are measured before and after a fixed amount of product is applied and allowed to equilibrate.

  1. BASELINE

    Test area + untreated control
    Oil · Moisture · White · Device-derived elasticity

  2. PRODUCT APPLICATION

    Fixed test area · Fixed dose ·
    Standardized spreading

  3. AFTER EQUILIBRATION

    Repeat three readings at a fixed time

STANDARDIZED CONDITIONS

22 ± 2°C   ·   50 ± 5% RH   ·   Fixed equilibration time   ·   Three repeated readings

All measurements follow the same SOP for repeatability and comparability.

SKIN SENSORY STANDARD

From Readings
to Indices

Device readings are converted into two reference-calibrated relative indices.

ΔX% =(Xafter − Xbefore)(Xbefore + 0.01)× 100
CX = ΔX%test − ΔX%control

LFITMRelative Lipid Film Index

LFI =100 × clip(CO − CO,Light)(CO,Rich − CO,Light), 0, 1

PGITMPredicted Glide Index

Ĝ = β0 + βOCO + βMCM + βWCW
PGI =100 × clip(Ĝ − 1)6, 0, 1

β coefficients are established through one-time sensory calibration.

HOW TO INTERPRET

LFI estimates relative light-to-rich lipid-film character. PGI estimates relative skin glide from the calibrated device model.
Neither index directly measures film thickness or friction.

A Sensory Profile—Not A Performance Score

Unlike SPF or PA, LFI and PGI do not indicate protection, efficacy or product quality.
They describe two independent dimensions of sensory character under standardized A&S test conditions.

LFI levels are shown qualitatively; numerical classification thresholds remain provisional.
A&S proprietary classification. Product profiles should be compared only when measured using the same standardized protocol.

One Framework. Different Sensory Targets.

LFI and PGI describe how a formula feels—not whether one product type is better than another.

For rinse-off cleansing, in-use glide and post-rinse skin feel should be evaluated separately. Test site, dose, water exposure, rinsing conditions and equilibration time must be standardized.
Masks, scalp or hair products, and color cosmetics require protocols adapted to their intended use.
