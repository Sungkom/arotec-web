Exposome Science

Understanding how environment, lifestyle, biology, and time shape human health, beauty, and wellbeing.

The Exposome is Built from Three Interacting Dimensions

The biological effect of exposure is not determined by one factor alone. It emerges from the interaction between external exposures, internal biological state, and exposure dynamics over time.

01

External Exposome

Environmental Exposures

All non-genetic exposures from the external environment.

  • Physical environment: air pollution, UV radiation, noise, temperature, light
  • Chemical environment: chemicals, heavy metals, pesticides, endocrine disruptors
  • Biological environment: pathogens, allergens, environmental microbiota
  • Built & social environment: urban space, indoor environment, occupation, social factors
  • Other exposures: microplastics, electromagnetic fields, water quality, food contaminants
02

Internal Exposome

Lifestyle & Individual Biological Factors

Endogenous and modifiable factors within the individual.

  • Diet / nutrition
  • Physical activity
  • Sleep
  • Stress
  • Smoking, alcohol, medications
  • Social interaction
  • Immune function
  • Inflammation and oxidative stress
  • Metabolism and hormones
  • Gut microbiome
  • Genetics / epigenetics
  • Neuroendocrine function
03

Exposure Dynamics

Quantifying Exposure Over Time

Exposure impact depends on how much, how long, how often, when, and in what combinations.

  • Intensity: how much exposure
  • Duration: how long exposure lasts
  • Frequency: how often exposure occurs
  • Timing: time of day, season, life stage, critical windows
  • Mixture / interactions: combined effects of multiple exposures
  • Cumulative burden: total accumulated exposure across life

05

EBII Framework

Exposome Biological Impact Index. EBII is a conceptual quantitative framework that integrates exposure load, biological susceptibility, adaptive capacity, and time to estimate overall biological impact. It is not a validated medical diagnostic score.

E

E — Exposome Load

The total burden of external and internal exposures.

  • External exposome: air, water, chemicals, UV, noise, diet, lifestyle
  • Internal exposome: metabolism, inflammation, microbiome, hormones, immune status
  • Exposure dosimeter: intensity, frequency, duration, route, timing, accumulation
S

S — Biological Susceptibility

Intrinsic factors that influence individual sensitivity.

  • Genetics
  • Epigenetics
  • Age and sex
  • Developmental stage
  • Disease status
  • Lifestyle factors
A

A — Adaptation / Adaptive Capacity

The capacity of the body to respond, repair, recover, and adapt.

  • Detoxification capacity
  • Immune resilience
  • Repair capacity
  • Neuroplasticity
  • Metabolic flexibility
  • Homeostasis maintenance
T

T — Time / Temporal Accumulation

The temporal dimension of exposure and biological response.

  • Duration
  • Frequency
  • Timing
  • Cumulative burden
  • Critical windows
  • Life course stage
06

How EBII Combines Load, Susceptibility, Adaptation, and Time

Higher exposure load, susceptibility, and temporal accumulation may increase biological impact. Higher resilience and adaptation may buffer and reduce the final impact.

Conceptual state function

EBII = f(E, S, A, T)

Approximate conceptual model

E × S × TR EBII ≈ numerator buffered by resilience
R

Biological resilience acts as a buffer and protective factor, reducing the final impact.

07

High EBII vs Low EBII

EBII helps explain how different combinations of exposure, susceptibility, time, and resilience can lead to different biological trajectories.

High EBII

Higher Biological Impact

  • High exposure load and/or susceptibility
  • Longer or greater temporal accumulation
  • Low resilience or poor adaptation
  • Greater biological impact and risk

Possible outcomes

Oxidative stress, chronic inflammation, cellular senescence, organ dysfunction, premature aging, reduced quality of life, and lower physical or cognitive function.

Low EBII

Lower Biological Impact

  • Low exposure load and/or susceptibility
  • Shorter or lower temporal accumulation
  • High resilience and strong adaptation
  • Lower biological impact and risk

Possible outcomes

Balanced inflammation, healthy cell function, optimal organ function, better vitality, healthy aging, higher quality of life, and longer healthspan.

08

EBII Impacts Across Multiple Biological Levels

Biological impact can accumulate from molecular changes to cellular dysfunction, tissue and organ decline, whole-body vulnerability, and long-term human outcomes.

Molecular Level

DNA damage, epigenetic alterations, oxidative stress, mitochondrial dysfunction, proteostasis disruption

Cellular Level

Cellular senescence, stem cell exhaustion, dysregulated inflammation, impaired signaling

Tissue / Organ Level

Neuroaging, cardiovascular decline, metabolic dysfunction, immune dysregulation, skin aging, gut dysbiosis

Whole Body Level

Physiological decline, reduced resilience, impaired recovery, lower adaptability, increased vulnerability

Human Outcomes

Healthspan, wellbeing, functional ability, quality of life, longevity

09

What Makes EBII Higher or Lower?

What Makes EBII Higher?

  • High exposure environment: pollution, toxins, UV, etc.
  • Unhealthy lifestyle: poor diet, lack of sleep, sedentary behavior, smoking, alcohol, chronic stress
  • High susceptibility: genetics, age, disease, epigenetics
  • Longer and cumulative time: chronic exposure, delayed recovery
  • Low resilience: poor detoxification, weak immunity, stress overload, poor adaptability
Higher EBII Lower EBII

What Makes EBII Lower?

  • Cleaner environment and reduced exposure
  • Healthy lifestyle: nutrition, exercise, sleep, non-smoking, stress control
  • Lower susceptibility or early prevention
  • Manage time and reduce cumulative burden
  • Stronger resilience: detoxification, immunity, repair, stress resilience, recovery
