Section 02

Biological Impact

How exposures enter the body, create biological effects, and translate into acute, chronic, developmental, reproductive, and long-term quality-of-life outcomes.

Exposure Sources

  • Air pollution
  • Chemicals
  • UV radiation
  • Heavy metals
  • Pesticides
  • Lifestyle factors
  • Pharmaceuticals
  • Nanoparticles

Entry & Absorption

  • Inhalation
  • Ingestion
  • Dermal contact
  • Injection

Biological Effects

  • Neurological oxidative stress, neuroinflammation, cognitive impairment
  • Cardiovascular inflammation, endothelial dysfunction, hypertension
  • Hepatic detoxification burden, oxidative stress, steatosis
  • Immune dysregulation, chronic inflammation, autoimmunity
  • Respiratory inflammation, oxidative damage, decreased lung function
  • Endocrine hormone disruption, metabolic imbalance, thyroid dysfunction
  • Gastrointestinal microbiome disruption, barrier dysfunction, inflammation
  • Reproductive hormonal imbalance, reduced fertility, developmental toxicity

Cellular and molecular effects include ROS, DNA damage, epigenetic alteration, mitochondrial dysfunction, protein misfolding, and cell death.

Health Outcomes

  • Acute effects irritation, allergic reactions, poisoning
  • Chronic effects chronic disease, organ dysfunction, metabolic disorders
  • Developmental & reproductive birth defects, delays, infertility
  • Cancer mutagenesis, tumor promotion, carcinogenesis

Reduced Quality of Life

Physical limitations, mental health issues, and reduced longevity may follow sustained biological impact.

Key Mechanisms

Oxidative stress, inflammation, DNA damage, endocrine disruption, immune dysregulation, and metabolic dysfunction.

Prevention & Mitigation

Reduce exposure, strengthen resilience, and apply protective interventions.

Section 03

EBII Framework

A quantitative model integrating exposure, susceptibility, adaptation, and time.

E

Exposome Load

External exposome, internal exposome, and exposure dosimeter.

S

Susceptibility

Genetics, epigenetics, age, sex, developmental stage, disease status, and lifestyle factors.

EBII =(E × S)A× T

Exposure load amplified by susceptibility, buffered by adaptive capacity, and accumulated over time.

A

Adaptation

Detoxification, immune resilience, repair capacity, neuroplasticity, metabolic flexibility, and recovery capacity.

T

Time

Duration, frequency, timing, cumulative burden, critical windows, and life course stage.

Higher E / Higher S

More exposure load and greater susceptibility increase EBII.

Higher A

Stronger biological resilience lowers EBII and is protective.

Longer / Greater T

More temporal accumulation increases EBII risk.

Section 04

Exposome Biological Impact Index

A quantitative framework for assessing lifetime exposome impact on human health.

E = Exposome Load

External exposome: air pollution, chemicals, UV radiation, climate, diet, lifestyle, psychosocial stress, sensory environment.

Internal exposome: metabolism, inflammation, oxidative stress, hormones, immune status, microbiome, neural activity, epigenetic regulation.

Exposure dosimeter: intensity, frequency, duration, timing, route, and accumulation.

EBII =E × S × TR

R = Biological Resilience

  • Detoxification capacity
  • Immune resilience
  • Repair mechanisms
  • Neuroplasticity
  • Metabolic flexibility
  • Recovery capacity
  • Antioxidant defense
  • Homeostasis maintenance

Biological resilience acts as a buffer and protective factor, reducing the final impact.

S = Biological Susceptibility

  • Genetics
  • Epigenetics
  • Age
  • Sex
  • Developmental stage
  • Disease status
  • Lifestyle factors

T = Temporal Accumulation

  • Acute exposure
  • Chronic exposure
  • Cumulative burden
  • Exposure timing
  • Critical windows
  • Life course stages

Section 06

High EBII vs Low EBII

EBII contrasts exposure burden and resilience to show how biological impact may shape healthspan, beauty, wellbeing, and longevity.

High EBII

Higher Biological Impact

  • High exposure load and/or susceptibility
  • Longer or greater temporal accumulation
  • Low resilience or poor adaptation
  • Greater biological impact and risk
VS.

Low EBII

Lower Biological Impact

  • Low exposure load and/or susceptibility
  • Shorter or lower temporal accumulation
  • High resilience or strong adaptation
  • Lower biological impact and risk

EBII Impacts Across Multiple Biological Levels

Molecular level, cellular level, tissue or organ level, whole body level, and human outcomes connect into one biological impact chain.

What Makes EBII Higher?

  • High exposure environment
  • Unhealthy lifestyle
  • High susceptibility
  • Longer cumulative time
  • Low resilience
EBII= (E S T) / R

Your EBII today shapes your health tomorrow.

What Makes EBII Lower?

  • Cleaner environment and lower exposures
  • Healthy lifestyle
  • Lower susceptibility
  • Manage timing and reduce cumulative burden
  • Stronger resilience

Key Takeaway

Lower EBII means lower biological impact, better health, beauty, wellbeing, performance, and longer healthspan.
