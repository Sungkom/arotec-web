A&S Explores Neuro Skincare Through One Question: How Does the Personal Exposome Shape Biology Through the Skin and Senses?

The skin is a vital interface where environmental factors, stress, scent, touch and lifestyle interact with the body through sensory receptors, neural pathways and cellular signaling.

By integrating Exposome, Sensory, Olfactory and Neural Pathway research with Bio-Responsive Ingredients, A&S studies how the skin senses, responds and adapts to external stimuli.

Cellular Responses in Skin

Cellular Responses in Skin

How Stress Signaling Alters Skin Cell Behavior

Neural, immune and endocrine stress signals reshape the behavior of multiple skin cell populations, progressively weakening the coordinated functions that maintain skin homeostasis.

Keratinocytes

Impaired Differentiation · Reduced Barrier Renewal

Fibroblasts

Collagen & Elastin ↓ · MMP Activity ↑

Melanocytes

Altered Pigmentation Signaling

Sebocytes

Altered Sebum Production · Increased Oxidation

Immune & Endothelial Cells

Inflammation ↑ · Vascular Reactivity ↑

Together, these cellular changes contribute to the loss of skin homeostasis.

BRAIN-SKIN AXIS

A CONTINUOUS FEEDBACK LOOP

The brain and skin communicate continuously through neural, endocrine and immune signals.
Stress-related signals from the central nervous system influence skin responses,
while sensory and inflammatory signals generated in the skin return to the brain.
This bidirectional feedback may maintain homeostasis—or contribute to its disruption
when stress becomes prolonged.

The brain and skin operate as a bidirectional neurocutaneous system. Central stress signals influence cutaneous biology, while skin-derived neuroendocrine and sensory signals feed back to the brain.

How Exposome Stressors Disrupt the Skin Barrier

A Progressive Biological Cascade From Exposure to Barrier Breakdown

Why the Skin Barrier
Matters

A healthy skin barrier retains moisture and protects against external threats. Exposome stressors weaken this defense, increasing water loss and the penetration of irritants, allergens and microbes. This drives inflammation, sensitivity and impaired repair.

  • Defense Compromised
  • Water Loss Increased
  • Inflammation Amplified

  1. Exposome-Induced Mediators

    Cortisol · Catecholamines ·
    Neuropeptides · Cytokines · ROS

  2. Action on Skin Cells

    Keratinocytes · Mast Cells ·
    Immune Cells · Fibroblasts ·
    Sensory Nerves

  3. Molecular & Biological Responses

    Lipid Synthesis ↓ · Structural Proteins ↓ ·
    Tight Junctions ↓ · NMF ↓ ·
    Inflammation ↑ · Dysbiosis ↑

  4. CORE BIOLOGICAL OUTCOME

    Skin Barrier Disruption

    Lipid Loss · Junction Breakdown ·
    NMF Deficiency · High TEWL ·
    Microbial Infiltration

  5. Clinical Consequences

    Dryness · Sensitivity · Itching ·
    Inflammation · Delayed Healing ·
    Premature Aging

  6. Vicious Cycle

    Barrier Disruption → Inflammation &
    Sensitivity → Increased Stress Perception
    → Neuroendocrine Activation →
    Barrier Disruption
